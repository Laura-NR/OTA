Brand · 03 · the three directions side by side · 11 Oct 2026

A, B or C: the same places, the three marks

The four surfaces a logo has to survive on this project, with each option dropped in at its real size: the customer web header at 28 px, the browser tab at 16 px, the business card at 50 %, and the admin sidebar on dark teal at 26 px. Nothing is retouched; the header and sidebar are the Home and A-Dashboard markup with only the logo swapped. The notes at the end say what each does well and where it breaks. Decision taken 11 Oct 2026: C was chosen and is live on every board; A is kept here for the record.

05 · Honest notes · what each does well, where it breaks, where it fits

The choice is yours; none of the three is wrong for this brand. What differs is where the brand will be seen most, and how much it should look like the place or like the people.

OptionDoes wellWhere it breaksFits best
A · The trail and the sun
line mark + wordmark
  • One line and one dot: distinct silhouette at every size, and it survives as a stamp, an embroidery or a one-colour print.
  • Light and friendly next to photography; the teal interface never competes with it.
  • Already drawn on all 113 boards and in every header, so it costs nothing more.
  • A 3.2-unit stroke at 16 px is 1.6 px: the gap between trail and sun closes and the favicon reads as a squiggle.
  • "Path plus sun" is the shared vocabulary of hiking and outdoor brands: distinctive in this room, not in the category.
  • The mark is wider than tall and sits low, so it fills a square tile (app icon, avatar) less well than B or C.
Screens first, print second. The safe choice, and the one the canvas already wears.
B · The fanlight
solid emblem + wordmark
  • The only one of the three that is specifically Cuban: a casa owner recognises it before reading a word.
  • Solid shapes: prints, embosses and laser-cuts cleanly; the strongest app icon and avatar; fills a square.
  • Reads in one colour (ink, cream or ochre) without losing its identity, because the structure is in the keystone and lintel.
  • Five cames vanish under 20 px, so a second three-pane cut exists for small sizes: two versions of one mark to govern.
  • Teal inside the mark sits next to the teal buttons of the interface; on the dark sidebar it must recolour to ochre and cream.
  • Half-round fans are a crowded genre (sunrises, awnings, arches); the lintel is what keeps it a window, and it is the busiest of the three next to photos.
Print, signage, the doorway sticker on every partner casa, merchandise. A brand that will be seen in the street.
C · The name, set once
wordmark + a monogram for tight spots
  • Nothing to learn: the name is the logo, and it is strongest exactly where the brand lives most, in email, documents and the website header.
  • Cheapest to apply: one typeface the canvas already uses, any size, one colour if needed, embroiders as text.
  • The ochre full stop and the terracotta v give it a signature without a picture, and the Fraunces display alternates are hard to imitate by retyping.
  • No picture to own: "v." at favicon size is a letter, and out of context reads as "version" or "versus".
  • Depends on one typeface and its WONK alternates (SIL OFL, so safe to use, but the wordmark must never be retyped and the display cut is not what the body text uses).
  • In the dark sidebar it competes with the navigation words, and it is the weakest social avatar of the three.
Email, documents, stationery, a brand that is mostly written. The elegant choice if the agency is the person on the other end of the line.

Whichever you choose: the files exist (brand/, brand/option-b/, brand/option-c/), the card and the QR centre follow, and the headers on the other pages are swapped in one pass after the decision, not before.
