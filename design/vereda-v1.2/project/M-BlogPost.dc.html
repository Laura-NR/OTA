Why the grid fails

Cuba's power plants are old, fuel is short, and demand peaks in the evening. The utility publishes a daily deficit and provinces take turns going dark. In 2026 planned cuts of 12 to 20 hours outside Havana are normal in the hot months.

Your hosts plan their day around it: cooking early, charging phones when the light is on, a torch by the bed. You will too, and it is easier than it sounds.

What a cut looks like from inside a casa

The schedule is usually known a day ahead. The fan stops, the fridge hums off, the street gets quieter. A generator starts within a minute and the essentials come back; a battery keeps lights and the router but not the air conditioning.

Evenings are the hard part. Our travelers' tip: be out. Dinner at a paladar with its own generator, music, a walk on the Malecón. The casa is for sleeping, and a fan on a generator is enough for that.

"Two nights without power, ten hours each. The generator kept the fan on and Ana messaged every morning. Honest, tiring, worth it."

Marion T., Lyon · Feb 2026 · verified

What a generator does and doesn't run

  • Runs: lights, fans, fridge, charging, router, usually 4–8 h a night.
  • Doesn't: A/C in most casas, electric showers, kettles.
  • Batteries and solar: quieter, smaller; 6–8 h of lights and charging.
  • Hot water: tanks heated when the grid is up. Shower early.

What we do before you arrive

  1. Only stays with verified backup power. It's a filter and a line on every stay card.
  2. A 20,000 mAh power bank per room and a headlamp in your kit.
  3. Important things in the morning, plus a daily WhatsApp check-in.
  4. If a cut runs past 12 h and the casa can't cope, we move you. Lost nights refunded pro rata, free rebooking within 12 months.

What you can do

  • Bring your own power bank and a torch; charge whenever there's light.
  • Download the offline kit and maps before you fly.
  • Cash in small notes; a shop without light still sells water.
  • Carry a printed itinerary and your emergency card.
  • Ask your host about the schedule each morning; plan the evening out.

Why it's still worth it

Because the outage is also when the street comes alive: neighbours on the porch, dominoes under one bulb, someone with a guitar. We won't romanticise it. But if you understand the reality before you go, you can handle the inconvenience; what you can't handle is being surprised. That sentence came from a traveler, and it is why this page exists.
