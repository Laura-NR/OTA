E-visa & entry

Four steps, start three weeks out

Last verified 20 Sep 2026 against the consular portal · reviewed monthly
1

Check your passport

Valid at least 6 months beyond your return date. Children need their own.

Today
2

Apply for the e-visa

Paper tourist cards ended on 1 Jan 2026. Apply on the official portal; approval usually within 72 business hours; single entry, 90 days, extendable once. Cost ≈ € 22–50 depending on your nationality and airline.

Complete D'Viajeros

Cuba's digital arrival form: passport, flight, address of your first casa (it's on your voucher), health declaration. Not earlier than 7 days before your flight. Save the QR to your phone and the offline kit.

4

At the airport

Show the e-visa code, the D'Viajeros QR, proof of insurance and your return flight. Keep € 300–500 in cash; nothing over the equivalent of US$ 5,000 undeclared.

Arrival

Dual nationals & Cuban-born travelers

Cuban-born travelers may need a Cuban passport or habilitación. Rules differ by case; tell us at booking and we'll point you to the consulate.

US citizens and residents

Entry to Cuba is the same e-visa. US law adds the travel-category certification: see the US travelers page. Note that an ESTA may be affected by a Cuba visit; check the US page before you book onward travel.

We help

Links, a checklist with your dates, and a reminder 21 and 7 days out. Upload the approvals in Documents and we check them before you fly.

Money & cash

Bring € 300–500 a week in cash. Not dollars.

Last verified 28 Sep 2026 · reviewed monthly

Cards

Don't work in Cuba

Visa and Mastercard stopped functioning on the island in June 2026. No ATMs, no card terminals. Plan for cash only.

Which notes

Euros or Canadian dollars

Clean, recent notes, mixed denominations. US dollars carry a penalty on exchange and are a sanctions trigger for some payments.

How much

€ 40–70 per person per day

Lunches and dinners out (€ 15–25), tips, taxis, souvenirs. Your package covers stays, transfers, guides and listed meals.

Before you go

  • Routine vaccines up to date; hepatitis A and typhoid advised; no yellow-fever requirement from Europe or Canada.
  • Bring your own medicines for the whole trip plus a week: pharmacies are short of basics.
  • Dengue and Oropouche circulate: repellent with DEET, long sleeves at dusk.

In Cuba

  • Drink bottled or filtered water; casas provide it, we add a filter bottle to your kit.
  • International clinics (Cira García in Havana) treat foreigners; provincial hospitals handle emergencies. Addresses are on your emergency card.
  • Ambulance 104 · Police 106 · Fire 105 · Vereda line 24/7 answers in 15 minutes and calls your insurer for you.

Connectivity

1–2 Mbps when it works. Plan to be offline.

Last verified 9 Sep 2026

Getting online

ETECSA tourist SIM or eSIM (buy before you fly or at the airport; data packages change price often). 4G in cities, 3G or nothing in the countryside. Wi-Fi in casas is usually a shared ETECSA card, slow and intermittent.

What works

WhatsApp and Telegram messages, light email, maps you downloaded. Video calls rarely. Many US-based services are blocked or refuse Cuban IPs; a VPN set up before departure helps.

How we stay in touch

Daily WhatsApp check-in each morning, when signal is best. If you don't answer by midday, your guide or host checks on you. Emergencies: the phone line works on any network, 24/7.

Cultural etiquette

You're a guest in someone's home, and country

Written with our hosts · reviewed yearly

In the casa

  • Say good morning. Hosts register your passport in a book the first night; it's the law, not suspicion.
  • Water and power are rationed: short showers, lights off, phones charged when the grid is up.
  • Breakfast is generous and included; dinner at the casa is usually the best meal in town, order it in the morning.
  • Tip at the end, in cash, directly; it goes further than anything you can buy.

Out and about

  • Ask before photographing people, and show them the picture. Our hosts and guides have agreed to appear on this site; strangers haven't.
  • Politics: people will talk if they want to. Don't push, don't lecture, don't post about individuals.
  • Dress is relaxed but churches, ballet and family dinners expect shoulders covered and no swimwear.
  • Jineteros (street hustlers) are polite and persistent: a friendly "no, gracias" works.

US travelers & OFAC

Legal travel to Cuba for US persons, step by step

Last verified 30 Sep 2026 · not legal advice · reviewed with counsel quarterly

Under the Cuban Assets Control Regulations, US persons may only spend money on Cuba travel under one of 12 general-licence categories, whatever the booking channel or payment method. Tourism as such is not one of them. Vereda is a Spanish company; the rules still apply to you.

1

Pick your category

Most of our travelers use Support for the Cuban People: a full-time schedule of activities with private hosts, farmers, artists and guides, avoiding restricted state entities. Family visits, journalism, professional research, religious and humanitarian work are others. As of 30 Sep 2026 the people-to-people group category no longer exists.

2

Certify at booking

A short form before payment: category, your name as on the passport, a typed signature. We store it for 5 years as the law requires and give you a PDF.

3

Travel the schedule

Your itinerary is the schedule. Keep it, your receipts and your voucher for 5 years. We don't book GAESA or Gaviota properties, nor anything on the US restricted list.

4

Pay in euros, outside the US banking system

US-issued cards are declined by our gateway. Pay by international wire or invoice. No transaction is cleared in US dollars.

Cuban-American family visits

"Family visits" covers close relatives in Cuba. Our Family Visit package is built for it: airport transfers, a casa near your family, luggage for supplies, Spanish-speaking desk. Remittance and gift rules are separate; ask us.

After the trip

A visit to Cuba can affect ESTA eligibility for some travelers to the US. Check the current State Department guidance before planning onward travel.

Not sure?

Three questions help you find a fitting category. If none fits, we'll say so rather than book you.
