Alerts you can hear, see and read; a matrix you can drive from the keyboard

The customer screens were annotated on DS sheets 02–05. This sheet does the same for the desk, Ops Lite and the worker app, and records a contrast audit of the status palette with two corrections that were applied across the canvas on 6 Oct.

Corrections applied on 6 Oct 2026

  • Focus ring. The DS specified 2 px ochre #F2B134 everywhere. On the cream ground that is 1.78:1, below the 3:1 required for focus indicators. Now: 2 px teal #0B4F6C, offset 2 px, on light surfaces (8.4:1 on cream, 8.9:1 on white); ochre stays on dark surfaces such as the sidebar (6.3:1).
  • Amber progress bars. #F59E0B on the #E8E2D9 track measured 1.67:1. Timer and availability bars now use #B45309 (3.9:1 on the track, 4.7:1 on cream); amber arrows in the handoff diagrams use the same. Amber pills keep their light fill and dark text (6.4:1), unchanged.
  • Colour was carrying the amber state alone on the dispatch matrix ("Offered · 1 h 31 m" in amber vs blue). The elapsed percentage is now written in the pill as well.

01 · Red and amber alerts

Three channels, one event

Audio

A short two-note cue when a red alert is raised, repeated every 60 s until acknowledged; amber gets one cue, no repeat. "Mute 15 min" is logged with the actor and never silences a new red alert. The cue is a desk setting, not a browser autoplay: it starts after the user's first interaction of the session.

Visual

The alert strip is red with an icon and words; the matrix row is tinted and its pill says "Timeout" or "Declined". Nothing blinks or scrolls (prefers-reduced-motion respected; there is nothing to reduce). Red and amber also differ in lightness and carry text, so they survive grayscale and deuteranopia.

Screen reader

The strip is a role="alert" region: a new red alert announces once, "Red alert. Booking VX-3PA8ZT, guide Osvaldo Ruiz timed out at 13:40. Press R to re-dispatch." Amber uses aria-live="polite". Timer ticks are never announced; only threshold crossings are.

02 · Dispatch matrix

A real table, read row by row

What a screen reader hears on the amber transport cell

row header "VX-7K2M9Q, Margaret Chen plus one, Eco-Tourism in Viñales, 13 to 20 February, 2 hour timer started 13:02."
cell "Transport. Offered to Reinier Soto, 1 hour 31 minutes elapsed, 76 percent, amber. Button: call plus 53 5 241 3388. Button: re-dispatch."
  • Column headers carry scope="col"; the booking code cell is the row header. Numbers are right-aligned in the mono face.
  • Tab order inside a row: Open → stay cell actions → transport cell actions → … Each action is a real button or link; the row itself is not clickable.
  • The timer bar has an aria-label with the percentage; the percentage is also printed in the pill (correction 3). Bars never carry information that is not also in text.
  • The inline re-dispatch panel opens below the row, receives focus on its heading, and returns focus to the "Re-dispatch" button on close. Offer buttons are labelled with the worker's name.
  • Under 1024 px the table scrolls inside its box and the first column is sticky; the sticky column never covers a focused control (focus not obscured, WCAG 2.4.11).

04 · Forms, controls, structure

The rules the desk screens follow

  • Every input has a visible label; search fields that look label-less carry a visually hidden one. Help text sits under the field, errors beside it and in a summary at the top (Edge-states sheet, 04).
  • Choice cards (categories, severities, re-dispatch candidates, proof decisions) are real <label> + radio pairs; state filters are buttons with aria-pressed; switches use role="switch" and aria-checked.
  • Disabled actions keep their label, stay focusable (aria-disabled) and show the reason beside them ("needs a second approver").
  • Targets: 44 px on the desk and Ops Lite, 48–56 px in the worker app; icon-only buttons carry aria-label.
  • Each page has one <h1>, sections have headings, the sidebar is a <nav aria-label="Admin"> with aria-current="page".
  • Tabs are role="tab" with aria-selected; arrow keys move between them, Tab leaves the list.
  • Language: the worker app and the recruitment page are lang="es"; Spanish quotes inside English screens (inbox, booking) are wrapped in lang="es" spans so speech switches voice.
  • Accessible authentication (WCAG 3.3.8): email link, passkey or SMS code; no memorised secret, no puzzle.
  • Redundant entry (3.3.7): the recruitment form carries the category into the credential step; the booking never asks a traveler's details twice.

05 · Worker app and Ops Lite

Outdoors, one-handed, often offline

  • No text under 4.5:1 and no thin grey on the worker screens; body 16 px, buttons 52 px, the emergency button 56 px with icon and words, never an icon alone.
  • Primary actions sit in the thumb zone above the bottom nav; destructive ones (decline, emergency) open a confirmation sheet with the reason first.
  • Offline: a persistent status pill plus a toast with aria-live="polite" for "guardado sin conexión"; nothing silently fails.
  • Countdowns are written as minutes left and announced only at amber and red; the timer bar duplicates, never replaces, the number.
  • Push text is duplicated by SMS after 60 min, and by a phone call after 1 h without connectivity (the desk sees the gap).
  • Ops Lite: the red card expands in place and moves focus to its heading; "Offer" buttons name the worker; the push notification uses the same words as the card.
  • Haptics on accept, decline and emergency; sound only for incoming offers, following the phone's silent switch.
  • Plain Spanish: no English jargon except the state names the desk uses ("PENDING_SYNC" appears once, explained).

06 · Charts and figures on A-Reports and the dashboard

  • Every bar and stacked bar carries an aria-label with its values ("Revenue by rail: SEPA 27,100, card 12,930, invoice 1,250"); the legend repeats the numbers in text.
  • The province heat list is a list, not a map; the funnel prints counts and percentages; sparkline-style month columns have a visually hidden table alternative in production.
  • Colour series are limited to teal, ochre and terracotta, which differ in lightness; red is reserved for alerts.
